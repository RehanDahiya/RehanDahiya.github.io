Week 4 · September 19, 2026

The Right Model for the Right Job

Key Learning Moments

This week was all about the tools people use to write code, though we did not write code ourselves. It was a review of the tools and how to use them well. We went through Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, and the VS Code setup, and compared where different models actually do well. The point that came through most clearly was that you should not limit yourself to one model. Different models are good at different things, and the job is to figure out which one fits the work in front of you. The professor went through this directly and pointed us toward which model makes sense for which kind of task, including local models that run on your own machine, not just the big hosted ones. We also looked at pricing, which most people skip and which matters a lot once you are actually paying for tokens.

One new thing for me was workflows. The professor showed n8n and how you can chain tools and models together into an automation that runs on its own, instead of doing every step by hand in a chat window.

We also covered how frontier models work at a high level and got into the theory of fine tuning: what actually changes inside a model when you fine tune it. The best moment of the week was a question from the class about whether you can fine tune ChatGPT. The answer was basically no, because it is a closed model and the weights are not available to you. You can only fine tune what you can actually get your hands on. That one question made the whole open versus closed model distinction click for a lot of people.

Personal Connections

This week matched how I already work more than any other week so far. I have been using multiple models for a while, one for coding, one for cheap background work, one for reasoning, and choosing based on what each is good at and what it costs. Hearing the professor lay it out as a method instead of a habit was useful, because it made me realize I had never actually written down why I use which model where.

The fine tuning part connected to something I have been thinking about for my Challenge project. I had a rough idea of taking a small open model and tuning it for one narrow job. This week gave me the theory behind why that only works with open weights, and why the small model has to be the one you tune while the closed frontier model stays as the thing you call.

Challenges and Growth

The honest gap is that I understand fine tuning in theory now but I have never actually done it. Knowing what changes in the weights and knowing how to run a training job on real data are very different, and I caught myself feeling like I knew more than I do. That is the same trap as last week with customer interviews, which I still have not done. Both of these are things I can only learn by doing, and this week made the list of things I have only read about a little more obvious.
