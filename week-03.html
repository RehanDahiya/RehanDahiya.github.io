Week 3 · September 12, 2026

You Are Not Your Customer

Key Learning Moments

This week was mostly about customers. Not the product itself, but who it is for, how it solves their problem, and how you actually go find those people.

One thing that changed how I think was this: a solution does not have to invent a completely new way of doing something. It can be for a problem that already has solutions. What matters is that it solves the problem in a simpler, easier, or just clearly better way. I used to think a good idea meant a new idea. Now I think a good idea means a better answer to a problem people already have.

The other big lesson was about who you ask. Your idea should not stay limited to yourself, your family, and your friends. They will hear it and say "yeah, that is crazy, great," because they like you, not because they need what you are building. You have to go out to people who do not know you, tell them "this is the problem and this is the solution we are thinking of," and let them tell you where you are lacking and where you are actually doing well.

Personal Connections

Both of these landed directly on my Challenge idea. There are already ways to check on an aging parent. People call, there are care apps, there is even a hardware companion device. So my idea is not new in the sense of the problem. What it has to be is simpler. No app to install, no device to buy, just a voice conversation on something older people already use. That is the "better, not new" idea in one sentence, and I would not have described it that way before this week.

The family and friends point hit even harder, because my plan for the first user was literally my own family. That is exactly the bias the professor described. My family will tell me it is amazing no matter what.

Challenges and Growth

The honest part is that I still have not talked to anyone outside my circle. I said in Week 2 that I would, and I have not done it yet. I know what I need to do now: find families who are not mine, in the same situation, and ask them the problem and solution question directly. The customer discovery workshop later this month is where I plan to actually start. Knowing the right step and taking it are two different things, and this week made that gap obvious.
