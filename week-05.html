Week 5 · September 27, 2026

Stories, Not Opinions

Key Learning Moments

This week was part review and part new. The review side was AI tools and automation again, plus the tools where you describe an app and get one built, like Bolt.new, Lovable, and Base44, and custom AI assistants like GPTs, Claude Projects, and Gemini Gems, which is what we built in Week 4.

The big one was customer validation. Not the idea that you should talk to people, which we have heard before, but how to actually do it. The feedback has to come from outside your immediate circle, not your parents or roommate, but people who live with the problem or know the field. The professor gave us the core questions an interview has to answer: do people actually experience the problem the way I think they do, would they want what I am proposing, and how do they handle it today and why does that not work.

Then he gave us the shape of the interview. Open with one or two questions that get the person talking about that part of their life. Then two questions on the problem. Then one or two testing the solution. Then close on why, would it matter, and what it would cost. The rule was 80 percent listening, 20 percent talking, and going for stories instead of opinions. The example on the slide was "wouldn't it be great if there was an app that could help you study better?" versus "tell me about the last time you studied for something important, how did it go?" The first gets a polite yes. The second gets a real story, and then you can bring up the app.

Personal Connections

This landed directly on what I did last week. For the Week 4 assignment I tested my elder care assistant with my mom and my dad. That is exactly the immediate circle the professor said does not count. Their feedback was still useful, but I asked them what they thought of the product, which is an opinion, not a story. Looking at the interview shape now, I skipped the opening and the problem questions and went straight to the solution.

Challenges and Growth

In Week 3 I wrote that I still had not talked to anyone outside my circle. Now I have talked to two people and they are my parents, so that is half fixed at best. What is different is that I now know what the conversation should look like. The next step is a few people who deal with elder care and do not know me, asked in this order, with me mostly listening.
