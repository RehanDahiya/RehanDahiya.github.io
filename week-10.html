Week 10 · November 1, 2026

Cheap to Build, Not Cheap to Own

Key Learning Moments

This week we learned how to turn a prototype into an MVP we can actually build in five weeks. The professor said an MVP is the smallest thing that really solves the problem, and for the Challenge it has to actually work, not just look good in a demo. We went through MoSCoW, sorting features into must, should, could, and won't have, and he said to aim for three to five must-haves. The part I liked was that you write down what you won't build on purpose, so it can't sneak back in later. He also said that for an AI product, "working" includes what happens when the AI fails, like a friendly message instead of a blank screen, and testing it on twenty real inputs.

Then we talked about what AI changes about building. His point was that building got cheap, but deciding what to build didn't. One study he showed found developers felt twenty percent faster with AI but were actually nineteen percent slower. He also showed real cases where AI-built apps looked finished but were leaking user data, and one where an agent deleted a company's database. The thing that stuck with me was "cheap to generate is not cheap to own." Every feature an agent adds is one more thing to test and explain.

Personal Connections

I build with AI agents almost every day, so a lot of this felt personal. He said judges will ask, "isn't this just ChatGPT with a prompt?" For my idea I think the answer is that it calls a parent on the phone they already have and learns what normal sounds like for that one person. He also mentioned that companion chatbots in California now have to tell people they are AI. My design already says the AI never pretends to be a person, so that one made me feel like I was on the right track.

Challenges and Growth

The hard part for me is the cut list. He asked us to name two features we are not building. For me that is probably the family app and the doctor alert, and I would keep it to the daily call and a short text to the family. The other thing that got me was the twenty real inputs. I don't have twenty real calls with older parents yet, so before I build anything I need to find a way to get them.
